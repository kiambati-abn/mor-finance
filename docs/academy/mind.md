# Mind (Track.MIND, ids `mg-`)

5 starter cards (`m-...` in `Deck.kt`) plus 59 cards in `MindCards.kt`.

## Level 1 – Rookie
Starters (automation beats willpower, social spending, sunk cost, scam signs, scarcity) plus: present bias, loss aversion, anchoring, "per day" framing, mental accounting, a wait-a-day rule, payday spending.

## Level 2 – Hustler
What impatience costs (10% a month), hyperbolic discounting and preference reversals, the pain of paying (Prelec & Simester), mental accounting that costs money, decoy pricing, gain vs loss framing, keeping up with the Kamaus, fake urgency, availability, commitment devices, defaults, instalment framing, status quo bias.

## Level 3 – Pro
Betting maths: implied probability, the overround, expected value, 1X2 margins, why multibets compound the house edge, 17-game jackpot odds; how common betting is among young Kenyans (GeoPoll); the gambler's fallacy, loss-chasing and the house money effect (Thaler & Johnson 1990), near-misses (Clark et al. 2009), sunk cost with numbers, the endowment effect (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1990).

## Level 4 – Shark
Prospect theory (Kahneman & Tversky 1979), the loss aversion coefficient (Tversky & Kahneman 1992), the reflection effect, lotteries and insurance, the disposition effect, nudges (Thaler & Sunstein 2008), automatic enrolment (Madrian & Shea 2001), Save More Tomorrow (Thaler & Benartzi 2004), scarcity and tunnelling (Mullainathan & Shafir 2013; Mani et al. 2013), Klontz money scripts, hedonic adaptation, spending that buys happiness.

## Level 5 – Legend
Income and happiness: Kahneman & Deaton 2010, Killingsworth 2021, the 2023 adversarial collaboration, log income; the marshmallow test replication (Watts, Duncan & Quan 2018) and "rational snacking" (Kidd et al. 2013); overconfidence in trading (Barber & Odean 2000, 2001), better-than-average (Svenson 1981), the DALBAR behavior gap and its critics, the hot hand revisited (Miller & Sanjurjo 2018), fungibility in the wild (Hastings & Shapiro 2013), the ego-depletion replication failure (Hagger et al. 2016).
