# Foundations (Track.FOUNDATIONS, ids `fb-`)

11 starter cards (`f-...` in `Deck.kt`) plus 59 cards in `FoundationsCards.kt`.

## Level 1 – Rookie
Starters (what a budget is for, pay yourself first, emergency fund size, inflation vs savings, compounding, rule of 72, 50/30/20, net worth, free till payments, two `you` cards on fees and the emergency target) plus: needs vs wants, tracking a month before budgeting, budgeting on net pay.

## Level 2 – Hustler
Zero-based budgets, 50/30/20 with real numbers, sinking funds and their maths, budgeting irregular hustle income on the low month, M-Pesa fee bands (one withdrawal vs four, free sends up to Ksh 100, tills vs Send Money), small daily leaks, fixed vs variable costs, irregular annual costs, who gains in a merry-go-round chama.

## Level 3 – Pro
Compound vs simple interest, monthly rates to yearly (EAR), the rule of 72 in reverse and where it fails, real return with current inflation, inflation erosion of cash, the three reasons for time value of money, opportunity cost in money and time, starting early (Wanjiku vs Otieno), the compounded cost of fees, paying off 30% debt as a guaranteed return.

## Level 4 – Shark
Personal balance sheet and cash-flow statement, net worth worked example, savings rate and years to freedom, a `you` card on savings rate, debt-to-income, the Employment Act two-thirds deduction cap, liquidity (why SACCO deposits aren't an emergency fund), liquidity and solvency ratios, depreciating assets on loan, automated money systems, founder runway.

## Level 5 – Legend
Present value, future value of an annuity and annuity due, PV of an income stream vs a lump sum, perpetuities and the freedom number, continuous compounding, the life-cycle hypothesis (Modigliani & Brumberg 1954), permanent income (Friedman 1957) and the evidence against it (Johnson, Parker & Souleles 2006), Campbell's "Household Finance" (2006), Lusardi & Mitchell's Big Three questions, the Fernandes, Lynch & Netemeyer (2014) decay finding and the Kaiser et al. (2022) rebuttal, Suri & Jack (2016) on M-Pesa and poverty.
