# Morph Academy: curriculum & cards

A daily 2-minute game inside Morph for understanding money deeply: personal finance, investing,
**financial freedom**, building businesses, and judging deals Shark-Tank style.

> Education, not financial advice. Cards teach concepts and *how to check* current numbers;
> rates and rules change, so anything time-sensitive is marked **(check current)**.
> Researched October 2026.

## How it plays (v2: a lifetime game)

Open it from the **💣 Today's Bomb** card on Home, or **Me → 🥋 Money Dojo**. With fun names off, these are **Today's lesson** and **Morph Academy**.

- **12 areas, one theme a day.** The areas rotate, so each comes round about every 12 days:

  | Area (fun name) | Plain name |
  |---|---|
  | 🧱 Pesa 101 | Money basics |
  | 👹 Madeni Monsters | Debt |
  | 🇰🇪 Kenyan Toolbox | Kenyan money tools |
  | 📈 Make Money Work | Investing |
  | 🦋 Freedom Road | Financial freedom |
  | 🚀 Boss Moves | Building a business |
  | 🦈 Shark Tank | Deals & valuation |
  | 🧠 Mind Games | Money psychology |
  | 🌍 Big Picture | Economy & markets |
  | 🛡️ Shield Up | Insurance, scams & safety |
  | 💼 Hustle Up | Earning more |
  | 🏠 Shamba & Keys | Land, homes & big buys |

  You can also tap any area to play it right now.
- **5 levels per area, and it gets harder as you play:** 🐣 Rookie → 🔧 Hustler → 🎯 Pro → 🦈 Shark → 👑 Legend.
  - Rookie covers basics.
  - Hustler applies them with simple numbers.
  - Pro has multi-step maths and trade-offs.
  - Shark covers what professionals use.
  - Legend goes into research findings, history and case studies.
  - A level opens once you **know** 60% of the one below. A card counts as known after you get it right twice.
- **Daily pace:** 2–3 new cards plus up to 3 reviews, about 2 minutes. This makes ~700 cards last about a year of new material. Reviews never stop: a card comes back at 1, 3, 7, 21, 60, 120, then 240 days, so it's a lifetime habit. Extra rounds ("💣 One more bomb") let you go faster.
- **Just in time:** if something just happened in your money (Fuliza, money in, high fees, a big spend, debts, no safety fund, low savings), a card about it jumps the queue, from any area.
- **Card types:**
  - **Quiz:** options are shuffled daily, so you learn the answer, not its position.
  - **Concept:** reveal the answer, then rate yourself.
  - **📊 Your numbers:** filled in from your own data.
  - Every fact shows its **📖 source**, and figures that change carry **⏳ Check current rates**.
- **👉 Try this** turns a card into one real step, often with an **Open →** button into Morph.
- **Rewards:**
  - Stars: +1 per card, +1 for a right quiz, +3 for the daily lesson.
  - A 🔥 streak.
  - Money IQ (0–100).
  - A level badge per area, with a celebration when you level up.
  - Your 🦋 freedom stage.
- **Fun names** run through the whole app: Pockets, Stash, IOUs to chase, Madeni Monsters, Money Rain, Must-pays, Treats & extras, Bahasha, mysteries to solve, jobless shillings, Money X-ray. Turn them off in **Me → Look & feel**. Search always understands both the fun and the plain names.

Each area's full outline and sources are in [docs/academy/](academy/).

## 1 · Foundations

1. **Concept: What is a budget really for?**
   Not restriction: a plan that gives every shilling a job *before* the month spends it for you. Morph's envelopes are exactly this.
2. **Concept: Emergency fund?**
   Money set aside only for real emergencies, enough for 3–6 months of *fixed* costs, kept somewhere safe and quick to reach (e.g. a money market fund). It's what stops one bad month from becoming debt.
3. **Concept: Inflation?**
   Prices rising over time, so the same shilling buys less. Kenya's target band is 2.5–7.5%; mid-2026 inflation was about 6.5% (check current). Money earning *less* than inflation is quietly shrinking.
4. **Concept: Compound growth?**
   Earning returns on your returns. Ksh 1,000 a month at 10% a year for 10 years grows to about **Ksh 205,000**, of which Ksh 120,000 is what you put in. Time does most of the work.
5. **Concept: Rule of 72?**
   72 ÷ yearly return % ≈ years for money to double. At 10%: about 7.2 years. At 6% inflation, prices double in about 12 years.
6. **Scenario: Pay yourself first.**
   Salary lands: Ksh 40,000. Option A: spend, then save what's left. Option B: move Ksh 6,000 to savings the same day, then live on the rest. *Which works?* B. Savings that wait for "what's left" rarely happen.
7. **Concept: Needs, wants, savings (50/30/20).**
   A starting guide: about 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving and debt repayment. Morph shows your real split in Insights.
8. **You: Your fees.**
   "You paid Ksh {fees_month} in M-Pesa fees this month. What's one change that cuts it?" (Pay tills instead of sending; fewer, bigger withdrawals.)
9. **Concept: Net worth?**
   Everything you own minus everything you owe. It's the scoreboard of wealth; income is just the speed.
10. **You: Your emergency fund.**
    "Your fixed costs are about Ksh {fixed_month} a month. 3 months of safety = Ksh {fixed_month×3}. You have {savings}. How many months is that?"

## 2 · Debt

1. **Concept: The real price of a loan.**
   Compare loans by the **total cost** (interest and all fees) per year, not by the fee per day. A small daily fee on a short loan can add up to a very high yearly rate.
2. **Concept: Good vs bad debt.**
   Good debt buys something that earns more than it costs (training that raises income, equipment for a profitable business). Bad debt pays for consumption that's gone tomorrow.
3. **Concept: Avalanche vs snowball.**
   Avalanche: extra money goes to the most expensive debt first (saves the most). Snowball: smallest balance first (quick wins for motivation). Either beats paying only minimums.
4. **Scenario: Fuliza habit.**
   You use Fuliza most months before payday. *What's the fix?* Build a small buffer (e.g. one week of spending) in M-Pesa or an MMF so you stop paying access and daily fees; Morph's Fuliza charges account shows the yearly cost.
5. **Concept: HELB.**
   A student loan with an interest rate and penalties for missed payments (check current terms). Paying on schedule protects your credit record; ignoring it gets more expensive.
6. **Concept: Credit reference bureaus (CRBs).**
   Lenders report repayment behaviour. A clean record means cheaper, bigger loans later, which matters a lot when you start a business.
7. **You: Your debts.**
   "You owe Ksh {debts}. Which one costs you most per year, and what monthly amount clears it in 6 months?"

## 3 · Kenyan money tools

1. **Concept: Money market fund (MMF).**
   A fund that pools many people's money into short-term, low-risk instruments (T-bills, bank deposits). Interest is calculated daily; withdrawals usually reach M-Pesa or the bank in about 1–3 working days. Funds must be licensed by the **Capital Markets Authority (CMA)**: check its approved list.
2. **Concept: MMF returns after tax.**
   Advertised yields have ranged roughly 8–11% a year (check current), and **15% withholding tax** applies to interest. 10% becomes about 8.5% after tax. Subtract inflation (~6.5%) and the *real* gain is about 2%. Good for safety, not for getting rich.
3. **Concept: Ziidi.**
   Safaricom's MMF inside M-Pesa (*334# or the M-Pesa app). It starts from Ksh 100, interest is daily, and moving money in and out is free. It's run with licensed fund managers and regulated by CMA. A Shari'ah-compliant version launched in October 2026.
4. **Concept: Treasury bills.**
   Short loans to the government: 91, 182 or 364 days. You buy directly through the CBK's **DhowCSD** app and pay with M-Pesa. Non-competitive bids start at Ksh 50,000 (check current).
5. **Concept: Treasury bonds.**
   Longer government loans (years) that pay interest twice a year. Some recent retail issues allowed Ksh 50,000 non-competitive bids, and bond interest has a withholding tax (check each prospectus).
6. **Concept: Competitive vs non-competitive bids.**
   A non-competitive bid accepts the average rate the auction sets. It's simplest for individuals. A competitive bid names your rate and may be rejected.
7. **Concept: SACCO.**
   A member-owned savings and credit co-operative. You can earn **dividends on share capital** and separate **interest on deposits**, and borrow (often a multiple of your deposits). Deposit-taking SACCOs are regulated by **SASRA**. Average dividends were about 10.5% for 2024 (check current).
8. **Concept: SACCO safety.**
   Check that it's SASRA-licensed, read its annual results, and don't keep everything in one SACCO. Some SACCOs have failed.
9. **Concept: Shares on the NSE.**
   Owning a slice of a listed company. You can earn dividends and price growth (or losses), and you need a CDS account through a stockbroker. Prices swing; it's long-term money only.
10. **Concept: Pensions.**
    NSSF plus optional personal pension schemes; contributions can lower your tax (check current rules). They're the boring engine of freedom: small amounts, decades of compounding.
11. **Scenario: Where should Ksh 20,000 of savings go?**
    Emergency fund not yet built → MMF. Emergency fund done and money not needed for 1 year → T-bill or bond. Done, plus 5+ years away → add diversified long-term investments.

## 4 · Investing

1. **Concept: Risk and return.**
   Higher possible returns come with bigger possible losses. Anyone promising high returns with "no risk" is selling something.
2. **Concept: Diversification.**
   Don't keep all your eggs in one basket: spread across types (cash, bonds, shares, property, business) so one failure doesn't sink you.
3. **Concept: Time horizon.**
   Money needed within 1 year stays safe (MMF). Money for 5+ years can ride ups and downs for higher growth.
4. **Concept: Fees matter.**
   1–2% a year sounds small, but over decades it can take a large slice of your result. Always ask: "What does this cost per year, all-in?"
5. **Concept: Dollar-cost averaging.**
   Invest a fixed amount every month regardless of price, so you buy more when it's cheap and stop trying to time the market. Ziidi's auto-invest works this way.
6. **Concept: Index funds.**
   Funds that simply own a whole market, so you get low fees and broad diversification. They're the default for long-term investors in many countries.
7. **Concept: Real vs nominal return.**
   Real return ≈ return − inflation. 12% in a year with 7% inflation is about 5% real growth in buying power.
8. **Scenario: "Double your money in 30 days."**
   A friend shows screenshots of profits. *What do you do?* Walk away. Guaranteed quick doubling is the signature of pyramid and Ponzi schemes; screenshots prove nothing.

## 5 · Financial Freedom

1. **Concept: What is financial freedom?**
   Having enough assets and passive income that work becomes a choice, not a requirement. It's not about being rich; it's about your money covering your life.
2. **Concept: The 5 stages.**
   1 · **Survival** (paycheck to paycheck, debt) → 2 · **Stability** (bills covered, small buffer) → 3 · **Security** (3–6 month emergency fund, no bad debt) → 4 · **Independence** (investments cover your basic costs) → 5 · **Freedom** (they cover the life you want). Know your stage; the next one is the goal.
3. **Concept: Your freedom number.**
   Yearly spending × 25 is the classic "FI number" (it comes from the 4% rule). Spend Ksh 600,000 a year → Ksh 15M.
4. **Concept: Where the 4% rule comes from and its limits.**
   US studies (the "Trinity study") found that withdrawing 4% a year, rising with inflation, usually lasted 30 years. But it's based on US market history and ignores fees and taxes. Research on emerging markets found 4% often wasn't safe there. For Kenya, a more careful plan is about 3–3.5%, i.e. ×30 or ×33.
5. **Concept: Savings rate beats income.**
   How fast you reach freedom depends mostly on the share of income you save, not the amount you earn. Rough guide (about 5% real return, starting from zero): save 10% → ~50 years; 20% → ~37; 30% → ~28; 50% → ~17; 70% → ~8.5.
6. **Concept: Assets vs liabilities.**
   An asset puts money in your pocket (a business, a rental, dividends, interest). A liability takes money out (a car loan, a phone on credit). Wealth is built by buying assets with income.
7. **Concept: Passive income ladder.**
   MMF interest → T-bills/bonds → dividends → rental income → business profits run by others. Each step needs more money, knowledge or time to build.
8. **Concept: Coast FI.**
   The point where your investments, left alone, will grow to your freedom number by retirement, so you only need to earn your living costs from then on. It gives early freedom of choice.
9. **Concept: Lean FI vs Fat FI.**
   Lean: freedom on a simple lifestyle. Fat: freedom with lots of room. Decide which one you want; it changes your number a lot.
10. **Concept: Sequence risk.**
    A big market drop in the first years of living off investments hurts much more than a later one. That's why freedom plans keep 1–2 years of spending in safe cash.
11. **Concept: Lifestyle creep.**
    When income rises and spending rises just as fast, freedom never gets closer. Rule: bank at least half of every raise.
12. **Concept: Human capital.**
    Early on, your biggest asset is your earning power. Investing in skills (and later a business) can move you up the stages faster than any fund.
13. **You: Your freedom number.**
    "You spent Ksh {spend_year} in the last 12 months. At ×30 your freedom number is Ksh {spend_year×30}. Your net worth is {net_worth}: that's {pct}% of the way."
14. **You: Your savings rate.**
    "Last month you kept {savings_rate}% of what came in. At that rate, freedom takes about {years} years. At +10% it takes {years2}."
15. **You: Your runway.**
    "If income stopped today, your savings cover {runway} months of fixed costs. Freedom stage: {stage}."

## 6 · Founder

1. **Concept: Problems before ideas.**
   Great businesses start from a painful, frequent problem that people already spend money or time on, not from a cool idea.
2. **Concept: The problem journal.**
   Write down every frustration you notice (yours and others'). Rate each: how painful, how often, how many people, would they pay? Your best idea is usually in there.
3. **Concept: Talk to customers.**
   Ask about their past behaviour ("When did this last happen? What did you do? What did it cost?"), not about your idea ("Would you use…?"). People are polite about ideas; their past behaviour is honest.
4. **Concept: Minimum viable product (MVP).**
   The smallest thing that tests whether people want it and will pay. Often it's a WhatsApp group, a form or manual service, before any app.
5. **Concept: Gross margin.**
   (Price − direct cost) ÷ price. Sell at 100, costs 60 → 40% margin. Low margins need huge volume.
6. **Concept: Break-even.**
   Fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit) = units you must sell to stop losing money. Rent 30,000, profit 50 per unit → 600 units a month.
7. **Concept: CAC and LTV.**
   CAC is what it costs to win a customer. LTV is the profit a customer brings over the whole relationship. Healthy businesses keep LTV at least 3× CAC.
8. **Concept: Cash flow beats profit.**
   A business can be "profitable" on paper and still die because cash arrives late. Track cash in and out weekly (Morph does this for you personally).
9. **Concept: Bootstrapping vs raising.**
   Bootstrapping means growing from customers' money: you keep control and grow slower. Raising means taking investors' money: faster, but you give up a share and take on pressure. Raise when money (not knowledge) is the bottleneck.
10. **Concept: Kenyan lesson: M-Pesa.**
    M-Pesa succeeded by solving a real, frequent pain (sending money home) through an existing behaviour (buying airtime from agents). Look for problems that ride on habits people already have.
11. **You: Your business fund.**
    "Your 🚀 Business fund is Ksh {fund}. With fixed costs of Ksh {fixed_month}, that's {months} months of runway to build."

## 7 · Deals (Shark Tank)

1. **Concept: Valuation from an ask.**
   "Ksh 2M for 10%" means the founder values the business at Ksh 20M (2M ÷ 0.10).
2. **Concept: Valuation vs profit.**
   A business making Ksh 1M profit a year, valued at Ksh 20M, is priced at 20× profit, which is expensive unless it's growing fast. Small, steady businesses often sell for a few times their yearly profit.
3. **Scenario: Mama mboga.**
   Profit Ksh 1,500 a day, about Ksh 540,000 a year. She asks Ksh 100,000 for 20% (a Ksh 500,000 valuation, about 1× yearly profit). *Good deal?* Cheap on paper, but is that "profit" really her own salary? If she leaves, does the business still earn? Value what the business earns *without* the owner's labour.
4. **Concept: Dilution.**
   Each time a company sells new shares, existing owners' percentages shrink. 100% → sell 20% → founder owns 80% → sell another 25% of the company → founder owns 60%.
5. **Concept: Equity vs debt vs royalty deals.**
   Equity: a share of the company forever. Debt: repaid with interest. Royalty: a % of sales until a cap is reached. Founders with steady sales often prefer royalty or debt so they keep ownership.
6. **Concept: Red flags in a pitch.**
   No paying customers yet; "no competitors"; numbers that don't add up; founder can't explain their margins; everything depends on one big client.
7. **Scenario: Weekly case template.**
   *What is the problem? Who pays? Margin? Customers so far? CAC vs LTV? Ask and valuation? Would you invest, counter-offer, or pass, and why?*

## 8 · Money mind

1. **Concept: Present bias.**
   We value today far more than tomorrow. Automation (auto-save, envelopes) beats willpower.
2. **Concept: Social spending.**
   Spending to keep up with friends or social media. Notice it in Morph's Friends & social and Fun categories.
3. **Concept: Sunk cost.**
   Money already spent shouldn't drive the next decision. Ask: "Knowing what I know now, would I start this today?"
4. **Concept: Scam patterns.**
   Urgency ("today only"), secrecy, guaranteed returns, and pay-to-join recruitment. Any one of them is a reason to stop.
5. **Concept: Abundance vs scarcity.**
   Under money stress people make short-term, expensive choices (like Fuliza and quick loans). Building a buffer first literally improves decision-making.

## How it's built

- `core/.../academy/Deck.kt` + `academy/deck/<Area>Cards.kt` (one file per area; builders in `deck/Build.kt`): every card, with its level (1–5) and source (id, track, kind, question, answer, options, the *Try this* step, signals, ⏳ flag). YOU cards use placeholders such as `{fees_month}`, `{savings}`, `{freedom_number}`, `{runway}` and `{stage}`.
- `core/.../academy/Academy.kt`: the rules (spacing boxes, daily theme, levels, today's session, mastery, Money IQ, streak, filling placeholders).
- `core/.../academy/MoneyFacts.kt`: turns your numbers into placeholder values, your freedom stage and the money signals.
- `app/.../data/AcademyData.kt`: reads those numbers from your ledger. `academy_progress` table (DB v5).
- Tests: `core/src/test/.../academy/AcademyTest.kt`.

To add a card, add one line to `Deck.kt` with a new unique id. Tests check the ids and quiz answers.
